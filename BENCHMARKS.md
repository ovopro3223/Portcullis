How to read this:

- Each row is the **median of 1000 timed batches**; a batch is a run of back-to-back parses sized to take at least about 100 microseconds, because one parse is far below the resolution of a clock read. These are throughput numbers (no data dependency between parses), not single-parse latency.
- 1 MB = 10^6 bytes. The parsers validate **structure** and never read payload bytes (a heartbeat payload, PNG `IDAT` data, TIFF value areas), so MB/s grows with the share of the sample that is payload. Compare parsers **on the same row's sample**; do not compare MB/s across formats. `ns / parse` is the number to read across formats.
- Run-to-run spread on a laptop is easily 10-25% (frequency scaling, other load). Read ratios from one run, and treat differences under that as noise.
- What each handwritten parser checks, and what it leaves to the caller, is written at the top of its header in `bench/handwritten/`. The Portcullis schema it is compared with must check the same things, or the ratio is not like for like.
